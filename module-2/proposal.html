Your Turn

Kira Dabbagh · Module 2: Interactive Devices · Project Proposal · October 15, 2026

Project idea and goals

For this project, I want to build a small physical controller that lets someone create a short dance on a laptop screen. I have been dancing ballet for eighteen years, so movement is something I connect with personally. I wanted to use that experience in a project that someone without dance training could also enjoy. Rather than asking visitors to perform ballet themselves, “Your Turn” will let them make choices for a dancer and watch those choices become a sequence.

The screen will show a simple stage and a stylized 2D dancer. The visitor will use a joystick to move a spotlight or marker to the dancer’s next position. Turning a potentiometer will select one of three movements: a plié, a turn, or a jump. The selected movement will be labeled on screen so the visitor does not have to recognize ballet terminology. Pressing a separate button will add the movement and its position to the choreography, and the dancer will perform that step as feedback.

After four choices, the program will play the whole sequence in order. It will then return to the selection stage so the visitor can create another dance. A small on-screen list will show the choices already made. My goal is for the controls to feel understandable through trying them: move to a place, choose an action, and press to add it. Each of the three required components will have a distinct purpose.

My main inspiration is my own experience learning ballet combinations, where individual movements become a phrase through their order and placement. The Lab 2 joystick exercise is my technical starting point: it already connects physical movement to a circle in Processing. I plan to extend that relationship into choosing positions on a stage. The project will focus on the experience of composing movement, rather than accurate simulation of a human body.

The ESP32 will read the joystick, potentiometer, and button through a breadboard circuit and send their values to the laptop over USB serial. Processing will interpret the readings and draw the stage and animations. To keep the project manageable, I will begin with one working movement before adding the other two and sequence playback. The dancer will use simple shapes and short animations so the interaction remains the priority.

Tasks to complete

  • Connect all three controls to the ESP32 and check each sensor’s readings separately.
  • Write the ESP32 sketch to send consistently formatted sensor values over serial.
  • Build the stage, position marker, movement selector, and four-step list in Processing.
  • Create recognizable plié, turn, and jump animations for the dancer.
  • Add button debouncing and stable knob selection so one action produces one clear choice.
  • Implement sequence storage, playback, and a reset for the next visitor.
  • Test with classmates, adjust confusing controls, and record the full interaction for the showcase.
  • Document the wiring with a Fritzing diagram and prepare the code, README, and final blog post.
